MARSWALK

        

          
On Earth, the blue dot on your phone's map comes from GPS satellites.

          
Mars has none.

          

            A compass does not work either: Mars has no global magnetic field. In 1971, the Apollo 14 astronauts
            had a good map of the Moon and still turned back about 30 m short of the crater they were looking for,
            because they could not tell where they were.
          

        

        

          
Your suit has Wayfinder. It puts you on the map without GPS:

          

            Always: it counts your steps and turns. Cheap, but it slowly drifts.

            When you can see far: a horizon scan matches the skyline around you with NASA's orbital
            elevation map, and corrects the drift.
          

          
Normally the rover's radio link would be your backup. Today it may not be.

        

        

          
EVA-1: The Lake Rocks

          

            Walk 700 m from the rover to Vera Rubin Ridge and collect a hematite sample. NASA's CRISM instrument saw
            hematite here from orbit; it often forms in water.
          

          
Forecast: a dust front may reach Gale Crater this afternoon.
