MARSWALK

        

          
On Earth, the blue dot on your phone's map comes from GPS satellites.

          
Mars has none.

          

            A compass does not work either: Mars has no global magnetic field. And you cannot call for directions,
            because a radio signal takes up to 22 minutes to reach Earth.
          

        

        

          
You do have a map. Orbiters have photographed Mars in great detail.

          
What you do not have is “you are here”.

          

            In 1971 the Apollo 14 astronauts walked toward a crater rim with a good map in hand. They turned back
            about 30 metres short of it, because they could not tell where they were.
          

        

        

          
Wayfinder is the suit's navigator. It works out “you are here” without GPS:

          

            it counts your steps, reads the sun, and takes bearings on landmarks you look at. It always shows how
            unsure it is.
          

          

            EVA-1: The Lake Rocks. Walk 700 m from the rover to Vera Rubin Ridge and collect a hematite
            sample. NASA's CRISM instrument saw hematite here from orbit; it often forms in water.
          

          

        

        
        

        

          Real terrain and imagery: NASA/JPL-Caltech/UArizona/USGS (MRO HiRISE). Minerals: MRO CRISM. Route: MSL Curiosity.
